# Formal argumentation — Pollock, Dung, ASPIC+

Pollock's defeasible reasoning (his OSCAR was a *personal* automated
defeasible reasoner; "rebutting" vs "undercutting" defeaters is his
vocabulary). Dung's abstract argumentation frameworks (1995) reduce
standing to a graph of attacks with grounded / preferred / stable
semantics; ASPIC+ and Carneades bridge structured arguments back to
those semantics.

**Gems:**

- Rebut (attack the claim) vs undercut (attack the inference) — the type
  system for defeat edges.
- Standing as graph semantics: acceptability is computed from attack
  topology, not stored per claim — a candidate definition for what the
  standing field *means*.
- Carneades: burden of proof and proof standards as explicit parameters,
  not implicit convention.

**Informs:** the defeat-edges QUESTION and standing's eventual
definition; `../user-stories.kb/reason-across-realms.md`.
