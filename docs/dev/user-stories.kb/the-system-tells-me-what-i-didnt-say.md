# The system tells me what I didn't say

Once my claims are down, I ask what follows from them: derived claims,
computed over the corpus and materialized where I can see them, marked
inferred rather than asserted. And constructively — a derivation hands me
the object it promises, a witness or a plan or a program, never a bare
verdict. Validation is not a sibling of this but its special case: the
inference whose extracted object is a proof that the corpus is
well-formed.

Asserted, and subordinate, by decree (INFER!): the system is procured to
infer, not only to record — but this purpose sits below everyday use and
may never tax speed-of-thought capture or the continuum between the
poles. The bet that inference and everyday use coexist in harmony is
SPAN!'s, and it is allowed to fail.

**Served by:** SPAN!, INFER!, SELF_APPLY, LAW (the special case is
already specified end to end — `corpus_ok` extracts a proof).

> [!QUESTION] where does the inferred layer live?
> Banked in the ledger as INFER_LAYER? (`host`). The generator runs
> surface→value; nothing yet runs value→derived claims. Unspecified:
> whether derived claims materialize into the corpus, how inferred is
> distinguished from asserted, and what keeps the inferred layer
> current. Gated by INFER!'s subordination; settles after everyday use
> is served.
